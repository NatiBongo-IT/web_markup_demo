About Ghana

Known as the Gateway to Africa, Ghana is a welcoming country on the coast of West Africa, famous for its history, its people, and its natural beauty.

A country of contrasts

Ghana stretches from sandy Atlantic beaches in the south to savannah plains in the north. Its capital, Accra, is a lively city of markets, music, and modern life.

In 1957 Ghana became the first country in sub-Saharan Africa to gain independence from colonial rule, a proud moment that still inspires the region.

Good to know

History

Ghana’s history includes powerful kingdoms, trade routes, and the long road to independence. Many visitors learn more through museums and heritage sites.

Climate and best time to visit

Ghana is warm all year. The dry season from November to March is usually the most comfortable time to travel, while the rainy season brings greener landscapes.

Language and people

English is the official language. Many people also speak local languages such as Twi, Ewe, and Ga. Hospitality is an important part of daily life.

Getting around

You can travel by domestic flights, buses, shared taxis (trotros), or ride-hailing apps in larger cities. Plan ahead for longer journeys.

Ghana military

Ghana’s armed forces help protect the country and support national security. When you travel, you may notice military presence at official sites and major events, reflecting discipline and public safety.

Ghana flag

The Ghana flag is a powerful national symbol. Each color represents important values, and the flag is commonly seen in public spaces, ceremonies, and cultural events— a reminder of unity, freedom, and identity.

Traditional clothing

Traditional Ghanaian clothing is known for bright colors, rich patterns, and cultural meaning. From kente-inspired designs to everyday garments worn during celebrations, you’ll see style that reflects heritage and community pride.

Ghana music

Music plays a major role in Ghanaian life. You’ll hear strong rhythms and storytelling in styles like highlife, as well as vibrant modern sounds. Festivals, radio, and live performances are great places to experience it.
